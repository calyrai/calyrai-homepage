SAXS: the model completes the measurement
SAXS DOI pending

Abstract

This talk isolates the core epistemic claim behind the SAXS program: scattering intensity does not uniquely define structure, and any reconstruction is conditional on a model that regularizes an underdetermined inverse problem. It states the Heisenberg-style intuition explicitly, not as a quantum claim, but as a Fourier-duality limit between reciprocal-space support and real-space resolution, and makes the causal point clear: this representational limit is why modeling is necessary in the first place. The presentation is intended as a methodological anchor for the SAXS branch, clarifying why structural interpretation has to be expressed in terms of admissible solution spaces rather than single naive inversions.
