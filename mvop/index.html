Workflow

From question to instrument booking in five steps

The agent doesn't just list instruments — it reasons about your scientific process, constructs a structured Method Object, and surfaces the facility that can actually execute it.

Step 01

Describe your problem

Free-text or structured input. Sample type, scale, constraints, prior data.

Step 02

Method proposal

Agent proposes two or three candidate methods with explicit trade-off reasoning.

Step 03

Method decomposition

Each method is decomposed into a typed object: setup, preprocessing, fitting, validation, failure modes.

Step 04

Facility matching

Method object is matched against the facility registry. Constraints filter unavailable instruments.

Step 05

Sequence + record

Agent generates an adaptive experimental sequence and logs the Method Object into the Nexus graph.

Method Objects

Methods as structured, executable objects

A Method Object is not a protocol PDF. It is a typed, versioned, executable graph node that constrains interpretation and enables reuse across experiments.

Solution SAXS — Rg & P(r) extraction
Structural biology · X-ray scattering
Measurement setup
SEC-SAXS inline; 1 mg/mL monodisperse protein; q range 0.01–0.5 Å⁻¹
Preprocessing
Buffer subtraction, Kratky check for foldedness, frame averaging
Fitting
Guinier (low-q), P(r) via GNOM; Rg, I₀, Dmax
Statistical assumptions
Spherical harmonic expansion valid; no inter-particle interference
Validation
Cross-validate Rg from Guinier vs P(r); compare to known MW from SEC
Failure modes
Aggregation artefacts, radiation damage, insufficient q-low coverage

I have monodisperse IgG antibody at 2 mg/mL. I want to know its hydrodynamic size and whether it's monomeric in solution.
Copilot
Good framing. Three methods are relevant here:

1. SEC-SAXS — gives Rg, P(r), and low-resolution shape simultaneously. Best if you also need structural information. Requires ~200 µL at ≥ 2 mg/mL.

2. DLS — fast hydrodynamic radius, but no structural detail. Good as a quick QC screen before a beamtime application.

3. AUC (analytical ultracentrifugation) — gold standard for sedimentation coefficient; distinguishes monomer/dimer rigorously but slower.

Given your stated goal of confirming monomericity, I'd recommend DLS first (1 h, low sample cost), then SEC-SAXS if you need structural confidence. Shall I generate a facility match for SEC-SAXS in Vienna?
